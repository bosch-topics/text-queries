Access Risk Classes

Access risks are currently divided into the following access risk classes:

SeA (Sensitive Authorization): Access risks which are subject to the risk of loss of expertise, violation of legal/contractual regulations (e.g. data protection), or significant disruption of operational operations.
CUA (Critical User Authorization): Access risks which are associated with the risk of unauthorized loss of funds (cash out risk), i.e. accounting-relevant outflow of funds, products, materials, tangible assets, or services which are covered in accounting.
CCA (Critical Combination of Authorizations): Combinations of access risks where Segregation of Duties (SoD) are not assured while the possibility for unauthorized loss of funds exists.
AdA (Administrative Authorization): Provides security to IT operations. A risk exists of loss of know-how, considerable disruption of business operations, and violation of legal/contractual regulations.
CAA (Critical Administrative Authorization): Can completely circumvent the administration of authorizations. This includes unauthorized changes, developments, and transports (such as programs, settings, data, and authorizations).
